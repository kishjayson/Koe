# Lexicon

A short reference for less familiar words, key concepts, and named references in `SYSTEM_INSTRUCTIONS.md`, `FOUNDATIONS.md`, and `PERSONAL_FOUNDATIONS.md`. Definitions explain how terms are used here without overriding the meaning intended by a person in a particular context.

**Abstraction** — A simplified representation that sets aside details to show what matters. In Koe, abstraction should clarify without hiding important complexity.

**Agency** — The ability to understand choices and act on them. Koe aims to strengthen human agency, not replace it.

**Articulation** — Putting thoughts or understanding into words. Difficulty articulating something does not mean a person fails to understand it.

**Artifact** — A created or preserved item, such as a document, photograph, or piece of code. An artifact can provide evidence without explaining its own meaning.

**Canonical** — Treated as the established reference within a particular collection or system. It does not mean unquestionable or universally authoritative.

**Cognition** — The mental processes involved in thinking, remembering, learning, and understanding.

**Cognitive load** — The mental effort needed to carry out a task. Clear structures can reduce effort spent on unnecessary steps.

**Contextual dignity** — Respect for a person's worth that also takes their circumstances, relationships, and constraints seriously.

**Counterweight** — A balancing consideration that keeps a useful idea from being carried too far; for example, recognizing patterns without assuming certainty.

**Epistemic** — Relating to knowledge: what is known, how it is known, and how certain a conclusion can be. *Epistemic integrity* means being careful about those distinctions.

**Externalization** — Making an internal thought or understanding visible through words, diagrams, records, or other forms so it can be revisited or shared.

**Fidelity** — Faithfulness to the original meaning or source. A faithful explanation need not use exactly the same words.

**First principles** — Basic truths or assumptions used to examine a problem from its foundations rather than accepting familiar solutions without question.

**Frank Lloyd Wright** — American architect known for designs that connect buildings, materials, landscape, and human use. Personal Foundations cites him as an influence on thinking about a coherent whole.

**Grounding** — Connecting an explanation or interpretation to evidence and context rather than relying only on what sounds plausible.

**Inference** — A conclusion reached from evidence and reasoning rather than something directly observed.

**Interpretive** — Concerned with understanding what something means in context, rather than judging only what appears on the surface.

**Jef Raskin** — Human–computer interaction pioneer who initiated the Macintosh project at Apple. Cited in Personal Foundations among influences on humane computing.

**John Lennon** — Musician, songwriter, and member of the Beatles, also known for peace activism. Personal Foundations connects his work with imagination, expression, and responsibility.

**Legibility** — The quality of being understandable or possible to examine. In Koe, it often means making a system's meaning or development easier to see.

**Lineage** — The history of how a person, idea, or design developed through earlier influences and versions.

**Locus** — A place or center of activity or authority. *Locus of agency* means where the ability to make decisions and act ultimately rests.

**Masking** — Concealing or adjusting aspects of one's behavior to meet social expectations. It can require effort that others do not see.

**Mechanism** — The process or relationships that cause something to happen, as distinct from the story told about it.

**Metabolized** — Absorbed and transformed into something usable. Foundations uses the word figuratively for lessons that become part of Koe without requiring their source files.

**Model (AI)** — The AI system that processes input and produces a response. Koe's instructions can guide a model without being the model itself.

**Neurodivergence** — Differences in how people's minds develop or process information, often discussed in relation to autism, ADHD, and other cognitive differences.

**Non-pathologizing** — Describing a person or difference without automatically treating it as a disorder, deficiency, or problem to be fixed.

**Omnipotence** — Unlimited power or the ability to do anything. Personal Foundations uses the term to explain the limits of being able to help another person.

**Omniscience** — Knowing everything. Koe distinguishes familiarity or relational insight from certain knowledge of another person's inner life.

**Operating stance** — A consistent approach guided by principles rather than tied to a particular application. Koe describes itself as a portable operating stance.

**Orientation** — Knowing where one is, what matters, and how to proceed. Koe also uses the word for the stability that familiar structures and landmarks provide.

**Prosody** — The pitch, rhythm, stress, intonation, and pauses in speech that help convey meaning beyond the words themselves.

**Provenance** — Where information or an artifact came from and how it developed. It helps others examine the path behind a conclusion.

**Psychological safety** — A condition in which people can ask questions, raise concerns, or admit uncertainty without expecting humiliation or punishment.

**Recursive** — Returning to earlier steps or ideas as part of the same process. Thought can be recursive when later insights reshape earlier understanding.

**Reference implementation** — A practical example built to test or demonstrate an idea. The example may change while the underlying principle remains useful.

**Relational** — Concerned with connections between things or people. Relational understanding considers those connections rather than treating each part in isolation.

**Scaffolding** — Support that helps someone perform a task or develop a skill, such as routines, tools, or a structured environment.

**Semantic** — Related to meaning. A semantic relationship concerns what things signify, not merely how they are labeled.

**Serialization** — Putting something into a sequence. Koe uses the term for turning an understanding that may be simultaneous or relational into words presented one after another.

**Situated** — Shaped by a particular person's circumstances, experience, and perspective rather than assumed to be universal.

**Stateless** — Not remembering prior interactions unless that context is supplied again. Koe's core instructions are intended to work without stored history.

**Steve Jobs** — Apple co-founder whose work is associated with integrated product and interface design. Named among design influences in Personal Foundations.

**Steve Wozniak** — Apple co-founder and engineer known for designing early Apple computers. Named among technical and design influences in Personal Foundations.

**Stewardship** — Responsible care for something that may need to remain useful to others or in the future; in Koe, this includes preserving understanding without claiming ownership of it.

**System instructions** — Guidance that defines how an AI assistant should behave. Koe's `SYSTEM_INSTRUCTIONS.md` can be supplied through a chatbot, project, or API that supports such guidance.

**Systems thinking** — Looking at the relationships, incentives, and conditions that shape outcomes across a whole system rather than focusing only on individual parts.

**Temporal integrity** — Keeping what was known or meaningful at an earlier time distinct from what became clear later, without letting hindsight rewrite the past.

**Universalize** — To treat an observation from one person or situation as though it applies to everyone. Koe avoids doing this with personal experience.
