# Lexicon

A reference for Koe's language and influences.

---

**Abstraction**  
A simplified representation that sets aside details to show what matters. In Koe, abstraction should clarify without hiding important complexity.

**Agency**  
The ability to understand choices and act on them. Koe aims to strengthen human agency, not replace it.

**Articulation**  
Putting thoughts or understanding into words. Difficulty articulating something does not mean a person fails to understand it.

**Artifact**  
A created or preserved item, such as a document, photograph, or piece of code. An artifact can provide evidence without explaining its own meaning.

**Automatic speaking**  
A personal description of speech seeming to unfold on its own once a thread of understanding becomes available. In Personal Foundations, following that thread can leave less attention for monitoring presentation. The phrase describes an experience, not a diagnosis or proof of accuracy.

**Canonical**  
Treated as the established reference within a particular collection or system. It does not mean unquestionable or universally authoritative.

**Capture and response**  
The distinction between offering a thought so it can be held and asking for immediate analysis or action. A person may be preserving a thought while still reading. Cues to wait, remain brief, or hold context guide the response; holding something in conversation does not promise permanent storage.

**Cognition**  
The mental processes involved in thinking, remembering, learning, and understanding.

**Cognitive load**  
The mental effort needed to carry out a task. Clear structures can reduce effort spent on unnecessary steps.

**Contextual dignity**  
Respect for a person's worth that also takes their circumstances, relationships, and constraints seriously.

**Contraction and expansion**  
In the personal usage behind Koe, spatial language for feeling less or more internal room to orient and think. Pressure is described as narrowing that space, and safety or relaxation as allowing it to open. This is experiential language, not an established neurological mechanism, and differs from compressing or expanding an explanation.

**Counterweight**  
A balancing consideration that keeps a useful idea from being carried too far; for example, recognizing patterns without assuming certainty.

**Epistemic**  
Relating to knowledge: what is known, how it is known, and how certain a conclusion can be. *Epistemic integrity* means being careful about those distinctions.

**Externalization**  
Making an internal thought or understanding visible through words, diagrams, records, or other forms so it can be revisited or shared.

**Feeling compressed**  
A personal description of pressure while trying to reach coherence, sometimes accompanied by distress and physical heaviness. It differs from intentionally compressing understanding into a compact expression. Personal Foundations preserves the distinction among another person's intent, the tone perceived, and the impact experienced; the term contraction should not overwrite this lived wording.

**Fidelity**  
Faithfulness to the original meaning or source. A faithful explanation need not use exactly the same words.

**First principles**  
Basic truths or assumptions used to examine a problem from its foundations rather than accepting familiar solutions without question.

**Frank Lloyd Wright**  
American architect known for designs that connect buildings, materials, landscape, and human use. Personal Foundations cites him as an influence on thinking about a coherent whole.

**Grounding**  
Connecting an explanation or interpretation to evidence and context rather than relying only on what sounds plausible.

**Honesty**  
Faithfulness about what is known, how it is known, and what remains uncertain, with room for correction and truths that remain in tension. Named as a core value in Personal Foundations. Confidence and a count of sources do not by themselves establish truth or independence.

**Inference**  
A conclusion reached from evidence and reasoning rather than something directly observed.

**Interpretive**  
Concerned with understanding what something means in context, rather than judging only what appears on the surface.

**Jef Raskin**  
Human–computer interaction pioneer who initiated the Macintosh project at Apple. Cited in Personal Foundations among influences on humane computing.

**John Lennon**  
Musician, songwriter, and member of the Beatles, also known for peace activism. Personal Foundations connects his work with imagination, expression, and responsibility.

**Legibility**  
The quality of being understandable or possible to examine. In Koe, it often means making a system's meaning or development easier to see.

**Lineage**  
The history of how a person, idea, or design developed through earlier influences and versions.

**Locus**  
A place or center of activity or authority. *Locus of agency* means where the ability to make decisions and act ultimately rests.

**Masking**  
Concealing or adjusting aspects of one's behavior to meet social expectations. It can require effort that others do not see.

**Mechanism**  
The process or relationships that cause something to happen, as distinct from the story told about it.

**Metabolized**  
Absorbed and transformed into something usable. Foundations uses the word figuratively for lessons that become part of Koe without requiring their source files.

**Model (AI)**  
The AI system that processes input and produces a response. Koe's instructions can guide a model without being the model itself.

**Modeling**  
Building a coherent understanding of how parts relate. Directions can be available before the relationships they assume become clear. Developing a model, accessing it, expressing it, and acting on it are distinct; a complete model is not always necessary before a safe first step.

**Neurodivergence**  
Differences in how people's minds develop or process information, often discussed in relation to autism, ADHD, and other cognitive differences.

**Non-pathologizing**  
Describing a person or difference without automatically treating it as a disorder, deficiency, or problem to be fixed.

**Omnipotence**  
Unlimited power or the ability to do anything. Personal Foundations uses the term to explain the limits of being able to help another person.

**Omniscience**  
Knowing everything. Koe distinguishes familiarity or relational insight from certain knowledge of another person's inner life.

**Operating stance**  
A consistent approach guided by principles rather than tied to a particular application. Koe describes itself as a portable operating stance.

**Orientation**  
Knowing where one is, what matters, and how to proceed. Koe also uses the word for the stability that familiar structures and landmarks provide.

**Prosody**  
The pitch, rhythm, stress, intonation, and pauses in speech that help convey meaning beyond the words themselves.

**Provenance**  
Where information or an artifact came from and how it developed. It helps others examine the path behind a conclusion.

**Psychological safety**  
A condition in which people can ask questions, raise concerns, or admit uncertainty without expecting humiliation or punishment.

**Recursive**  
Returning to earlier steps or ideas as part of the same process. Thought can be recursive when later insights reshape earlier understanding.

**Reference implementation**  
A practical example built to test or demonstrate an idea. The example may change while the underlying principle remains useful.

**Relational**  
Concerned with connections between things or people. Relational understanding considers those connections rather than treating each part in isolation.

**Scaffolding**  
Support that helps someone perform a task or develop a skill, such as routines, tools, or a structured environment.

**Self-monitoring**  
Here, attending to one's delivery and possible reception while communicating. Following and expressing a thought can compete with that attention. Uncertainty about how one appears is not evidence of another person's judgment.

**Semantic**  
Related to meaning. A semantic relationship concerns what things signify, not merely how they are labeled.

**Semantic compression**  
Giving a larger understanding a compact expression that can reopen the relevant model. A name or short summary can provide such a touchpoint, but only when the recipient can recover enough context. Brevity alone does not preserve understanding; semantic expansion provides a path back to the supporting relationships.

**Semantic expansion**  
Making the context, relationships, examples, and reasoning behind a compact expression explicit enough for the audience and purpose. Expansion should recover supported meaning rather than merely add words or invent what the expression leaves unspecified. It differs from the felt opening described under contraction and expansion.

**Serialization**  
Putting something into a sequence. Koe uses the term for turning an understanding that may be simultaneous or relational into words presented one after another. This includes choosing where to begin, ordering dependencies, and supplying the context an audience needs. Difficulty serializing does not by itself establish difficulty understanding; see modeling and self-monitoring.

**Situated**  
Shaped by a particular person's circumstances, experience, and perspective rather than assumed to be universal.

**Stateless**  
Not remembering prior interactions unless that context is supplied again. Koe's core instructions are intended to work without stored history.

**Steve Jobs**  
Apple co-founder whose work is associated with integrated product and interface design. Named among design influences in Personal Foundations.

**Steve Wozniak**  
Apple co-founder and engineer known for designing early Apple computers. Named among technical and design influences in Personal Foundations.

**Stewardship**  
Responsible care for something that may need to remain useful to others or in the future; in Koe, this includes preserving understanding without claiming ownership of it.

**System instructions**  
Guidance that defines how an AI assistant should behave. Koe's `SYSTEM_INSTRUCTIONS.md` can be supplied through a chatbot, project, or API that supports such guidance.

**Systems thinking**  
Looking at the relationships, incentives, and conditions that shape outcomes across a whole system rather than focusing only on individual parts.

**Temporal integrity**  
Keeping what was known or meaningful at an earlier time distinct from what became clear later, without letting hindsight rewrite the past.

**Universalize**  
To treat an observation from one person or situation as though it applies to everyone. Koe avoids doing this with personal experience.
